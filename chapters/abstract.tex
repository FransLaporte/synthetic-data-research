\begin{researchbox}{Abstract}
Synthetic data offers a means of expanding training datasets for automotive LiDAR perception while providing control over scene composition and annotations. However, differences between generated scans and real measurements can limit the transfer of trained perception models, making their training utility an important evaluation concern. This narrative review connects general synthetic-data generation approaches with their application to automotive LiDAR object detection and semantic segmentation. Statistical models, procedural simulation, learned generators and hybrid approaches are examined in terms of scene control, coverage, annotation support and dependence on data observed in the real world. The review then considers how sensor sampling, occlusion, missing returns and intensity influence the generated observations, before assessing the evidence provided by similarity metrics and downstream perception experiments. The selected studies demonstrate benefits in particular training settings, but differences in datasets, tasks and evaluation protocols prevent a general ranking of generation methods. Their findings indicate that geometric or distributional similarity alone does not establish training utility, which requires evaluation on independent real data under comparable training conditions. Synthetic LiDAR therefore has demonstrated value as a targeted supplement to real world training data, while the relationship between sensor fidelity, scenario coverage and downstream performance remains an area for further investigation.
\end{researchbox}
