\subsection{Practical trade-offs and contrasting evidence}
The evidence supports targeted supplementation of real data. When labels
and scenario control are central, scene-based simulation has a structural
advantage because object identity and placement are available before
rendering. When the objective is scan completion, a learned conditional
generator directly addresses the task. These are interpretations of the
reviewed mechanisms, not measured cost rankings. Real recordings, asset
construction, annotation mapping, trained networks and computation remain
part of the cost even when generating additional scans is inexpensive.

Realism and useful variation are related but distinct objectives.
LiDARsim supports the value of improving sensor sampling within its
pipeline, while LiDARGen's metric disagreements show that visual quality
and distributional matching can rank methods differently. SynLiDAR shows
that adapting a synthetic domain can improve training utility, and the
paired-scenario study shows that aggregate perception agreement does not
fully explain downstream planning behaviour
\parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020,zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022,
xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022,manivasagamZeroDomainGap2023}.
Together, these findings challenge a single realism score as a universal
selection rule. They do not imply that physical accuracy is unnecessary.

Coverage creates a further tension. Matching common real scenes can
underrepresent the difficult cases that motivated synthesis. Conversely,
oversampling rare scenarios changes the training distribution. We therefore
propose reporting both aggregate utility and performance on the explicitly
targeted conditions. An average improvement should not be assumed to
benefit every class or distance range.

\subsection{Research direction and limitations}
A defensible next question is which inexpensive sensor or distributional
diagnostics track real-data training gains under controlled LiDAR
perturbations. Existing work already evaluates transfer and paired simulator
realism, so the gap is not an absence of evaluation methods. The narrower
opportunity is to connect their levels: vary one factor, such as ray drop
or angular sparsity, keep scenes, labels, model and training budget fixed,
and compare changes in diagnostics with class- and range-specific real
performance. Multiple seeds and a separate real evaluation set would help
distinguish systematic effects from training variation. This is a proposed
extension; its novelty requires a broader literature search.

The present review is limited by a selected source collection, incomplete
database screening and pending independent author verification.
It cannot establish exhaustive coverage, the latest best method or
publication-bias estimates. The studies also differ in label sets, sensors,
geography, training budgets and evaluation purpose. Their reported gains
are not a pooled effect or a guarantee of deployment performance.
Compute costs and uncertainty intervals have not been systematically
extracted. Completing the search protocol, checking evidence records and
evaluating the proposed diagnostic-to-utility connection remain necessary
steps beyond this narrative synthesis.
