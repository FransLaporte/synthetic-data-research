\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This section weighs sensor fidelity against scenario coverage, identifies
an unresolved evaluation problem and considers the limits of the evidence.
PreSIL, LiDARsim and SynLiDAR demonstrate benefits under different training
protocols, supporting method selection around a defined task
\parencite{hurlPreciseSyntheticImage2019,manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020,
xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}. Scene-based methods provide object and
annotation control, learned models capture scan patterns, and hybrids connect
these capabilities; their real-data dependencies remain part of that choice.

\subsection{Sensor fidelity and scenario coverage}

Two complementary strategies address the domain gap: matching real sensor
behaviour and expanding the variation observed during training. LiDARsim's
learned ray drop and SynLiDAR's PCT support fidelity improvements in their
respective settings, whereas domain randomisation aims to broaden conditions
rather than reproduce each observation precisely
\parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020,xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022,
nikolenkoSyntheticDataDeep2019}. The reviewed evidence is more direct for
these particular fidelity interventions than for an automotive LiDAR
comparison of fidelity against randomisation. It therefore supports combining
coverage with sensor modelling, without identifying a universally preferable
balance or treating realistic scans as sufficient evidence of useful training.

Coverage also requires attention to rare cases. Shumailov et al. show loss
of distribution tails during recursive synthetic-data training in language,
VAE and mixture-model settings \parencite{shumailovAIModelsCollapse2024}.
This motivates provenance tracking and scenario-specific evaluation; its
recursive setting differs from one-pass LiDAR augmentation. The practical
question is which measurement changes matter for performance within the
scenarios that a dataset actually covers.

\subsection{Research gap: diagnostic sensitivity and training utility}
\label{sec:research-gap}

The selected studies leave unresolved how sensitive data-level diagnostics
are to sensor changes relative to the training utility of the same data.
LiDARGen reports disagreement between similarity metrics, while paired-scenario
analyses examine sensor discrepancies and autonomy-system outcomes
\parencite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022,manivasagamZeroDomainGap2023}.
Neither comparison, as used here, measures the ray-drop level at which FRD,
BEV MMD or JSD reacts against the level at which a model trained on those
scans loses class- or range-specific performance on real data.

A controlled experiment could vary uniform and range-dependent ray drop
while fixing scenes, labels, model and training budget, then compare diagnostic
changes with real-data segmentation outcomes. This connects the review to
the research proposal and would identify sensitivity or blind spots within
the tested conditions. The gap is bounded by the selected literature rather
than a claim of exhaustive novelty; diagnostic agreement would still require
validation before replacing downstream evaluation.

\subsection{Limitations and implications}

The focused source selection and incomplete systematic screening leave
uncertainty about coverage of relevant or contradictory studies. Differences
in sensors, tasks and protocols prevent pooling scores, while incomplete
resource-cost and repeated-run reporting limits efficiency and robustness
comparisons. Several studies also report validation outcomes rather than
independent final tests. Targeted source checks are documented in Appendix~\ref{sec:methodology};
these are distinct from a systematic review with independent duplicate screening.
Within these limits, the evidence supports targeted supplementation whose
contribution is measured on real data, leading to the conclusions below.
