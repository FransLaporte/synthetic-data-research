\section{LiDAR generation and requirements}
\label{sec:rq2}

LiDAR generation must connect scene construction, sensor observation and
annotations. Table~\ref{tab:lidar-generation-comparison} summarises how the
reviewed pipelines address these requirements. Their differences explain
why visually plausible outputs are not interchangeable training resources.

\subsection{Virtual scenes and reconstructed assets}

Virtual-scene pipelines generate labels alongside geometry, whereas
reconstruction-based pipelines reuse recorded assets and their coverage.
PreSIL derives annotated scans from GTA~V depth data, avoiding simplified
game collision geometry. Its forward-facing scans nevertheless omit
reflectance and motion distortion \parencite{hurlPreciseSyntheticImage2019}.
SynLiDAR provides virtual scenes with 32 semantic classes, but uses real
SemanticKITTI data for intensity modelling. Its point cloud translator
(PCT) separately addresses appearance and sparsity differences
\parencite{xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}.
These examples show that generated labels do not eliminate sensor-model
limitations or real-data requirements. Source classes must also be mapped
to those of the target perception task.

LiDARsim instead reconstructs maps and vehicles from real recordings.
Figure~\ref{fig:lidarsim-pipeline} separates asset construction from sensor
modelling: the upper panels show accumulated observations refined into a
\gls{surfel} asset, while the lower panels compare ray-cast, learned-ray-drop
and real measurements \parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020}.
The distinction matters because reconstructing a surface does not determine
which returns a sensor records. Scene coverage also remains constrained by
the available assets; the demonstrated reconstruction supports rigid
vehicles, with deformable road users requiring further modelling.

\subsection{Learned scans and shared requirements}

Learned scan models capture measurement patterns rather than explicitly
assembling labelled objects, which changes their control and annotation support.
LiDARGen learns range and intensity distributions in \glspl{range-image}
and supports conditional \gls{densification}. Its KITTI-360 and nuScenes
experiments use sensor-specific angular representations
\parencite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022}.
This captures measurement structure without automatically supplying
semantic labels or editable objects. Completion also retains information
from an input scan, so it must be distinguished from generating new
training examples.

Across these pipelines, scene diversity determines what is observed,
whereas sensor modelling determines how it is sampled. Paired-scenario
experiments identify material response, scanning motion and participant
geometry as contributors to the \gls{domain-gap}
\parencite{manivasagamZeroDomainGap2023}.
Scene coverage, sensor behaviour and annotation consistency must therefore
be assessed together. The next section examines how these properties are
related to useful perception performance on real measurements.
