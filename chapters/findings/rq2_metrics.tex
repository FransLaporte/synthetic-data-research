\subsection{RQ2: LiDAR generation and sensor requirements}
\label{sec:rq2}
\subsubsection{Scene-based and hybrid simulation}
LiDARsim separates asset construction, scene composition and sensor
rendering. Static maps and vehicle assets are reconstructed from repeated
real observations; objects and sensor poses can then be arranged into
scenarios. Ray casting determines surface intersections, while learned
\gls{ray-drop} models missing returns
\parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020}.
Figure~\ref{fig:lidarsim-pipeline} illustrates these two dependencies.
The approach supplies geometric control and object information for labels,
but inherits the coverage and reconstruction limits of the recordings.
Its vehicle-focused object construction does not establish equivalent
modelling quality for deformable road users.

SynLiDAR instead collects scans from virtual environments, providing
point-wise labels for 32 semantic classes and over 19 billion points.
Its intensity prediction uses real SemanticKITTI data, and its point
cloud translator (PCT) treats appearance and sparsity differences separately
\parencite{xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}.
Thus, simulated labels can reduce manual annotation of generated scans,
but the full pipeline is not necessarily independent of real data.
For transfer, source labels must also be mapped to the target class set.

\subsubsection{Learned scan generation and completion}
LiDARGen learns the distribution of range and intensity images, then
generates scans by stochastic denoising. Its angular representation ties
points to sensor rays and supports conditioning on partial observations.
The experiments include unconditional generation and \gls{densification} of
sparse scans on KITTI-360, with further generation results on nuScenes
\parencite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022}.
This avoids manually specifying every scene asset, but a plausible scan
does not automatically supply semantic ground truth or an editable scene.
Densification also retains information from its input scan; success there
cannot be counted as evidence that independently generated scenes improve
training. Training-data coverage and iterative sampling remain resource
constraints.

\subsubsection{Which requirements matter?}
The paired-scenario study by
\textcite{manivasagamZeroDomainGap2023} examines sensor effects using
reconstructions of recorded scenarios. It identifies motion blur, material
response and traffic-participant geometry as important contributors to
simulation discrepancies, and examines missing and multiple returns.
Its use of real observations as an oracle diagnoses what a simulator
should reproduce; it is not itself a general-purpose generator requiring
no reference scan.

Across these examples, sensor realism requires more than matching point
count: angular sampling, range-dependent sparsity, visibility, material
response and motion determine where measurements occur. Scene diversity
concerns the configurations observed, whereas sensor diversity concerns
how they are sampled. Our synthesis is that both must be specified.
Procedural scenes offer direct scenario and label control; learned scans
fit observed regularities; hybrids connect explicit geometry with
data-driven sensor effects. RQ2 is therefore answered by a division of
responsibility between scene construction, observation modelling and
annotation production. None alone guarantees the other two.
