\subsection{RQ2: LiDAR generation and requirements}
\label{sec:rq2}
\textbf{Virtual-scene simulation.} PreSIL produces annotated scans from
GTA~V, avoiding simplified collision geometry through its depth-based
simulation. Its released point clouds have a forward-facing field of view
and omit reflectance and motion distortion
\parencite{hurlPreciseSyntheticImage2019}. This provides scalable labels
without reproducing every sensor property. SynLiDAR supplies virtual scenes
with 32 semantic classes; its intensity model uses real SemanticKITTI data.
Its point cloud translator (PCT) treats appearance and sparsity differences
separately \parencite{xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}.
Consequently, automatic synthetic labels do not eliminate all real-data
requirements, and source classes must be mapped to the target task.

\textbf{Reconstruction and hybrids.} LiDARsim composes scenes from real
maps and vehicle assets, renders them and learns missing returns
(Figure~\ref{fig:lidarsim-pipeline}). It offers geometric control but inherits
asset coverage and reconstruction limitations; its vehicle construction
does not establish equivalent support for deformable road users
\parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020}.

\textbf{Learned scan generation.} LiDARGen generates range and intensity
images through stochastic denoising and supports conditional
\gls{densification}. Its KITTI-360 and nuScenes experiments connect
generation to sensor-specific angular representations
\parencite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022}.
However, plausible scans do not automatically provide semantic labels,
editable scenes or independently generated training examples when inputs
are reused.

Across these approaches, RQ2 requires scene construction, observation
modelling and annotation production to be considered together. Geometry
alone is insufficient: paired-scenario experiments identify material
response, scanning motion and traffic-participant geometry as contributors
to the \gls{domain-gap}
\parencite{manivasagamZeroDomainGap2023}.
Scene diversity describes what is observed; sensor diversity describes
how it is sampled. Neither can substitute for the other.
