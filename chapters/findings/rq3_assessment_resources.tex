\subsection{RQ3: Evaluation and transfer to real driving data}
\label{sec:rq3}
\label{sec:lidar-evaluation}
\subsubsection{Geometry, distributions and coverage}
For paired reconstruction or completion, geometric or range errors compare
a generated observation with its corresponding real scan. They require
compatible alignment, visibility and sampling. For unpaired generation,
matching an arbitrary real scan would penalise legitimate scene variation;
distributional measures are more appropriate.

LiDARGen compares spatial distributions using \gls{mmd} and \gls{jsd}, and
learned scan features using \gls{frd}. Its spatial metrics use
bird's-eye-view (\gls{bev}) histograms; FRD uses RangeNet++ features
\parencite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022}.
These measures capture different properties. On KITTI-360, ProjectedGAN
has the lower MMD in Table~1, whereas LiDARGen has the lower range-feature
distance and divergence. On nuScenes, the authors report a concentration
of generated points near the viewpoint despite favourable visual
comparisons. This disagreement is evidence against treating a single
similarity score as a complete quality judgement. Feature extraction,
projection, sample size and density must accompany the metric; an
image-based \gls{fid} cannot simply be assumed to measure LiDAR utility.

Coverage additionally requires checking relevant classes, distances,
occlusions and scenarios. Matching the average distribution can conceal
rare-case omissions. Annotation consistency must be assessed after
translation or augmentation as well as before it: a label is useful only
if it still describes the generated observation.

\subsubsection{What the real-data experiments establish}
Performance should be reported on real observations excluded from training.
Validation data support model selection; a final held-out test set supports
evaluation after selection
\parencite{raschkaModelEvaluationModel2020}.
Synthetic validation scores alone cannot demonstrate real-world transfer.
For detection, report the relevant \gls{ap}, class, overlap threshold and
whether boxes are evaluated in 2D, bird's-eye view or full 3D.
For segmentation, \gls{miou} summarises class overlap; class mapping and
ignored labels are essential. Metric definitions are provided in the glossary.

LiDARsim's public experiments use real validation data: Table~1 reports
vehicle/background segmentation on SemanticKITTI, and Table~2 reports
bird's-eye-view vehicle detection on KITTI's hard split. At \gls{iou}~0.7, the
reported detection score is 73.7 for LiDARsim, 42.2 for modified CARLA and
80.0 for real KITTI training
\parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020}.
These results support transfer for that protocol, not a full 3D detection
or multiclass segmentation claim. The setup also adjusts sensor geometry
and uses KITTI labels to build its dynamic object bank, which qualifies
any interpretation of synthetic training as eliminating real-data costs.

SynLiDAR provides a complementary augmentation comparison.
Tables~2--3 report the scores reproduced in Table~\ref{tab:transfer}.
The improvements establish utility in those experiments, while PCT's
additional gains include a translation intervention and cannot be
attributed to more synthetic samples alone
\parencite{xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}.

LiDARGen's downstream example instead runs an existing RangeNet++ on
densified scans without \gls{fine-tuning}. Its reported IoU increases from
0.394 with nearest-neighbour upsampling to 0.449 with LiDARGen
\parencite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022}.
This supports its completion application, not the claim that training on
unconditional generated scans improves a perception model. Keeping these
settings separate prevents superficially similar scores from becoming
a misleading generator ranking.

\subsubsection{Assessment resources and validity}
A useful training comparison includes real-only, synthetic-only, mixed
training, and synthetic pretraining followed by real fine-tuning where
applicable, all evaluated on the same real data. Our proposed controls are
a fixed model and training budget, documented real-label access, repeated
seeds and class-, range- and scenario-specific results. Adjacent scans,
reconstructed maps and source assets can introduce overlap, so independence
must be checked at scene or sequence level across generator training,
perception training and evaluation. Record sensor calibration, class
mappings, feature-network checkpoints, preprocessing and computation;
these are prerequisites for reproducing the comparison.

Simulator evaluation has a separate objective. The paired-scenario work
evaluates an already-trained autonomy system and finds that aggregate
detection precision and recall need not closely indicate the motion-planning
domain gap \parencite{manivasagamZeroDomainGap2023}.
Sensor diagnostics and system-level testing are therefore complementary
to perception utility, but neither demonstrates gains from retraining.

In answer to RQ3, task-appropriate performance on held-out real data is
the most direct indicator of training usefulness. Its change against a
controlled real-only baseline should be the primary comparison.
Distributional and geometric measures diagnose discrepancies and coverage;
no universal proxy-to-utility relationship is established by this evidence.
