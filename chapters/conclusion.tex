\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Synthetic generation can support autonomous-driving perception by producing
controlled, labelled scenes, fitting the statistics of measured scans, or
combining explicit simulation with learned sensor effects. RQ1 identifies
statistical, procedural/physical, learned and hybrid approaches, whose
trade-offs concern control, coverage, real-data dependence and computation.
RQ2 shows that LiDAR generation additionally requires an observation model:
geometry, beam sampling, visibility, missing returns and annotation
consistency must be considered together.

For RQ3, performance on held-out real data is the most direct evidence of
training utility. The selected studies demonstrate useful transfer in
specific settings, while also showing why similarity scores, completion
results and simulator testing cannot be substituted for that evidence.
A practical assessment should combine controlled real-data baselines with
sensor diagnostics and class- or scenario-specific analysis. The review
therefore supports targeted use of synthetic data alongside real data,
with method selection guided by the intended task and available resources.
Establishing which diagnostics reliably predict such gains remains a
focused research direction, subject to broader literature verification
and controlled experiments.
