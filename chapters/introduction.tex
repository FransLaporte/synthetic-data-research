\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Training perception models requires observations and labels that represent
the conditions in which the models will operate. Collecting and annotating
automotive \gls{lidar} \glspl{point-cloud} is costly, while uncommon traffic
configurations are difficult to capture at scale. \Gls{synthetic-data}
offers control over scene composition and access to simulated ground truth.
However, differences between generated and real measurements can limit
transfer to the intended task
\parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020,xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}.

This review first maps generation methods for \gls{machine-learning}, then
focuses on scene-level automotive LiDAR for \gls{object-detection} and
\gls{semantic-segmentation}. It distinguishes new-scan generation from
reconstruction-based simulation and completion of an existing scan.
Object-only shape generation is relevant only where it supports driving
scenes. The broad survey literature provides context; the LiDAR studies
provide the task-specific evidence.
