\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Autonomous-driving perception uses \gls{lidar} measurements to identify
objects and surfaces through tasks such as \gls{object-detection} and
\gls{semantic-segmentation}
\parencite{geigerAreWeReady2012,behleySemanticKITTIDatasetSemantic2019}.
Developing these methods requires annotated \glspl{point-cloud}, but
labelling real observations is labour-intensive. SemanticKITTI required
more than 1,700 hours of labelling, verification and correction
\parencite{behleySemanticKITTIDatasetSemantic2019}, while uncommon traffic
situations are difficult to capture in sufficient numbers through recording alone.
\Gls{synthetic-data} offers opportunities to expand training datasets,
construct controlled scenarios and generate annotations
\parencite{hurlPreciseSyntheticImage2019,xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}.

Its usefulness depends on more than scene geometry. LiDAR samples visible
surfaces from a particular viewpoint, with sensor sampling, \gls{occlusion}
and missing returns shaping the observation. Differences between generated
and real scans can consequently limit perception performance
\parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020}.
Furthermore, plausible scans do not necessarily provide suitable training
data. Scan completion, distributional similarity and improvements from
synthetic-data training represent different forms of evidence
\parencite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022,xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}.

This narrative review examines selected literature on synthetic-data
generation for scene-level automotive LiDAR object detection and semantic
segmentation. Its scope includes virtual-scene simulation,
reconstruction-based simulation and learned scan generation. Completion
is considered where it clarifies a method's capabilities, while its
results are distinguished from synthetic-training experiments.
The review first compares general generation approaches, then examines
LiDAR-specific sensor and annotation requirements. It subsequently assesses
evaluation metrics and real-data transfer before discussing limitations
and further research. The starting point is how generation choices shape
control, coverage and dependence on real observations.
