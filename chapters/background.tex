\section{Background and terminology}
\label{sec:background}
\subsection{Generation, simulation and augmentation}
Synthetic data are observations produced by a computational process rather
than directly recorded for the dataset. A generated sample can nevertheless
depend on real training data or reconstructed assets. Simulation specifies
how a scene produces an observation; learned generation estimates a sampling
process from examples. \Glslink{augmentation}{Augmentation} modifies or
recombines existing observations. These categories overlap: a reconstructed
vehicle can be inserted into a new scene and rendered with a learned sensor
component \parencite{nikolenkoSyntheticDataDeep2019,manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020}.
Here, synthetic-only training describes the perception model's inputs,
not the absence of real data anywhere in their production.

\subsection{A scene is not a sensor observation}
A LiDAR scan samples visible surfaces along emitted beams. It does not
represent a complete three-dimensional scene: \gls{occlusion}, the angular
sampling pattern, range and missing returns determine which surfaces appear.
\Gls{intensity} also depends on surface and sensor response. A spinning
sensor acquires its scan over time, so motion can distort geometry
\parencite{manivasagamLiDARsimRealisticLiDAR2020,manivasagamZeroDomainGap2023}.
Consequently, realistic object shapes alone do not establish realistic scans.

Representations expose different structure. Point sets retain coordinates,
\glspl{range-image} organise returns by direction, and \glspl{voxel} group
space into cells. Range images
support regular image-like processing but depend on sensor projection and
resolution. LiDARGen uses range and intensity channels, whereas SynLiDAR's
segmentation experiments use voxel-based processing
\parencite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022,xiaoTransferLearningSynthetic2022}.
New-scene generation, rendering another viewpoint of a reconstructed scene,
and filling gaps in an existing observation must therefore be assessed as
distinct tasks.

\subsection{Realism, coverage and utility}
We use realism for physical plausibility, similarity for agreement under
a specified distributional measure, and coverage for representation of
relevant classes and conditions. \Textcite{alaaHowFaithfulYour2022}
separate fidelity, diversity and generalization through $\alpha$-Precision,
$\beta$-Recall and Authenticity; the last concerns copying training data,
not downstream perception generalization. These concepts motivate multiple
evaluation dimensions without establishing a validated LiDAR metric.
\Gls{utility} denotes performance on the intended downstream task.
The synthetic-to-real \gls{domain-gap} is a difference between generated
and target data that may affect that performance. Distribution matching,
paired geometric accuracy and real-data utility consequently answer
related but non-equivalent questions.
