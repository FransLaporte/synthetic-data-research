## Findings, highest priority first

1. **High: conclusions exceed the experiment.** `proposal/chapters/hypothesis.tex:10` and `proposal/chapters/plan.tex:5` claim that reaction-point agreement supports replacing downstream evaluation, and that a worse diagnostic justifies rejecting a dataset without training. A threshold comparison on one dataset, architecture and degradation family cannot establish that decision rule for unseen data. The diagnostic reaction is two-sided, so even an improvement can trigger it. Reframe the contribution as evidence about diagnostic sensitivity within the tested conditions; independent validation would be required before using a score as a replacement decision rule.

2. **High: calibration does not guarantee degradation.** `proposal/chapters/methodology.tex:29` says matching average empty-pixel fractions ensures every further level moves synthetic data away from real data. Matching that one statistic does not match range-, class- or scene-conditional distributions or guarantee a monotonic decline in performance. Keep calibration as a limited occupancy adjustment and explicitly permit non-monotonic outcomes. `proposal/chapters/plan.tex:87` also switches both reaction points to two-sided definitions, contradicting the one-sided performance-drop definition in Section 5.5. Retain a distinct decline endpoint and report improvements separately.

3. **High: reaction-point uncertainty is insufficiently specified.** `proposal/chapters/methodology.tex:36,44-46` compares a degraded point estimate with the baseline interval, without propagating uncertainty in the difference. Scans in driving sequences are also temporally dependent. Specify paired resampling of baseline/degraded synthetic scenes with a common real-reference resample, using sequences or justified temporal blocks; estimate intervals for the score difference. State whether these are descriptive pointwise intervals or adjust for the multiple levels/diagnostics. The three-seed CV rule is an operational threshold, not a guarantee against normal variation. Define zero/near-zero baseline handling, outcomes that never cross within 40%, and how non-monotonic crossings affect lags. Do not assign a finite lag when a reaction point is unobserved.

4. **High: declarations are unfinished.** `proposal/chapters/conditions.tex:6-10` contains TODOs and visible Dutch contribution placeholders, while asserting both authors approve the final version. Replace these only with confirmed factual contributions and a truthful AI-use statement. Do not invent authorship details or claim approval before review is complete.

5. **Medium: hypothesis evaluation is incomplete.** `proposal/chapters/hypothesis.tex:6-8` leaves the small-class set and the treatment of mixed class outcomes unclear. H3 predicts the largest lag for small/distant cases, but its stated refutation criterion only tests the sign of the overall lag. Predefine the mapped classes and range-band comparisons, handle absent classes, and assess each part of the hypothesis rather than treating mixed evidence as a binary result.

6. **Medium: terminology obscures the outcome.** `proposal/chapters/introduction.tex:8` and `proposal/chapters/methodology.tex:41` call the per-class outcome mIoU. Report overall mIoU, per-class IoU, and mIoU within each range band, specifying the averaged class set and zero-union convention. Keep the distinction between Jensen-Shannon divergence and its square-root distance consistent throughout.

7. **Medium: feasibility and novelty remain provisional.** The architecture, subset size, epoch budget and start/end dates are not fixed; `proposal/chapters/plan.tex:89` explicitly acknowledges an undocumented novelty search. Record the search before claiming the gap is established. Add a concrete compute budget and dated milestones. If scans, epochs or seed counts change after baseline runs (`plan.tex:81`), rerun the baseline under the final protocol and recompute the threshold before evaluating degraded runs. The eight-week experiment should be distinguished from the 6 November proposal submission deadline.
