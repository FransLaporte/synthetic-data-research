\section{Hypotheses}\label{sec:hypotheses}
This section states an expectation for each sub-question, and together these expectations form an anticipated answer to the main question. The hypotheses are expressed in terms of two reaction points on the degradation scale. The mIoU drop point is the lowest degradation level at which the mIoU falls noticeably below its value for the calibrated baseline ($d=0$). The diagnostic reaction point is the lowest level at which a diagnostic moves outside its normal variation, and both points are defined precisely in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}. Each hypothesis is stated together with the outcome that would refute it, so that the experiment can clearly support or contradict it.

\textbf{H1 (SQ1).} FRD reacts to both forms of ray drop, whereas BEV MMD and BEV JSD react to uniform ray drop at a higher degradation level than to range-dependent ray drop, or not at all within the tested range. As described in Section~\ref{sec:scope}, uniform ray drop removes every point with the same probability, whereas range-dependent ray drop removes distant points with a higher probability than nearby points. In the LiDARGen implementation, both BEV diagnostics are computed on histograms that are normalised to sum to one, so that each cell holds the fraction of all points of a scan that falls within it~\cite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022}. Uniform ray drop removes, in expectation, the same fraction of points from every cell, which leaves these fractions unchanged and only makes the histograms noisier as fewer points remain. Range-dependent ray drop removes relatively more points from distant cells and therefore shifts the fractions towards cells close to the sensor. FRD is instead computed from RangeNet++ features of range images, in which every removed point appears as an empty pixel regardless of its position, and is therefore expected to respond to both forms. H1 is refuted if the BEV diagnostics react to uniform ray drop at the same level as or earlier than to range-dependent ray drop, or if FRD does not react to one of the two forms within the tested range.

\textbf{H2 (SQ2).} The mIoU of small and distant objects declines at a lower degradation level than the overall mIoU. Small objects such as persons, bicyclists and poles are represented by few points, and the point density of a LiDAR scan decreases with distance. A model trained on degraded data therefore observes these objects with even fewer points, which makes their shape harder to learn, while large and nearby surfaces such as road and buildings remain well represented. This effect is expected to be stronger for range-dependent ray drop, since that form removes distant points with a higher probability. H2 is refuted if the mIoU of small objects and of the far-range band declines at the same level as or later than the overall mIoU.

\textbf{H3 (SQ3 and main question).} Each diagnostic reacts at a higher degradation level than the mIoU, and this lag is largest for the class- and range-specific mIoU of small and distant objects. The diagnostics summarise complete scans, which are dominated by frequent classes and nearby points. A decline that is concentrated in small and distant objects, as expected under H2, therefore affects only a small share of the points that determine the diagnostic score. This expectation is in line with earlier indications that such diagnostics are an incomplete indicator of downstream performance, since they rank generative models differently~\cite{zyrianovLearningGenerateRealistic2022}, and that detection metrics averaged over a complete dataset do not necessarily reflect the behaviour of the full autonomy system~\cite{manivasagamZeroDomainGap2023}. H3 is refuted for a diagnostic if it reacts at the same level as or earlier than the overall mIoU.

Together, the hypotheses describe an expected pattern in which the diagnostics react later than downstream performance, most strongly for uniform ray drop and for the classes and distance ranges that synthetic data is intended to supplement. If this pattern is confirmed, a favourable diagnostic score would be insufficient grounds for omitting real-data evaluation when degradations are subtle. The reverse inference would remain valid, however, since a clear change in a diagnostic would then indicate that downstream performance has already declined, so that a dataset version rated worse by the diagnostics could be rejected without training a model. If instead the diagnostics react in step with the mIoU, this would support their use as a less costly substitute in both directions. The method described in Section~\ref{sec:method} is designed to distinguish between these outcomes.
