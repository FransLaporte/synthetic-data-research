\section{Problem and objective}
This project will map synthetic data generation methods for machine learning,
then investigate their use for creating LiDAR point clouds for autonomous
driving perception. The central issue is how generation choices relate to
sensor realism, scene diversity, annotations and performance on real data.

The broad stage provides a conceptual taxonomy across modalities. The
focused stage concerns scene-level automotive LiDAR for 3D object detection
and semantic segmentation. It distinguishes virtual-world simulation,
reconstruction-based novel-view synthesis, learned scene generation and
completion or augmentation. These tasks will not be treated as equivalent.

\section{Research gap and expectations}
The candidate gap is whether current comparisons adequately connect
generation methods, LiDAR-specific requirements and evidence of downstream
utility. This is a question to investigate, not a claim that such guidance
is absent. Existing surveys and primary studies may already answer parts
of it; the final gap will follow from the screened evidence.

The fidelity, diversity and generalization framework of
\textcite{alaaHowFaithfulYour2022} provides evaluation concepts to examine,
alongside sensor properties, annotations and real-test utility. We will
investigate whether better generation scores correspond to better
perception performance, without assuming that relationship in advance.

The planned contribution is a method--requirement--evidence matrix with
conditional conclusions about applicability and limitations.
Training a new generator or conducting a benchmark is outside this review.
